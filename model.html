Layer 2 · Agricultural Solution

Cattle as compounding, income-producing assets.

Veterans own breeding cattle. Qualified ranchers operate them. A defined offspring-share returns breeding stock to the veteran each cycle. Existing feedlot, corn and ethanol infrastructure bridges the launch.

The Four-Step Cycle

From acquisition to compounding.

1

Acquire

Breeding heifers sourced through a combination of domestic purchases and — only where regulatory validation succeeds — imports from approved disease-free regions abroad.

2

Hold & Clear

Controlled U.S. facilities handle entry, quarantine, testing, vaccination, feeding and monitoring where appropriate — using existing capacity before anything new is built.

3

Lease

Veteran-owned cattle deployed to qualified ranchers under standardized lease and herd-management agreements — custody, husbandry, breeding and operations.

4

Calve & Compound

An agreed offspring-sharing formula returns breeding stock to the veteran each completed cycle — growing the veteran’s asset base and the nation’s herd.

The Offspring Share

The engine of both veteran and national value.

The framework’s working example — approximately 20 calves/heifers returned per 100 leased cattle — is exactly that: a starting point for modeling, explicitly labeled illustrative until ranching economics, breeding rates and lease benchmarks are validated.

What the share must cover

  • Heifer conception and calving rates
  • Calf mortality pre- and post-weaning
  • Rancher compensation for custody and labor
  • Feed, veterinary and insurance costs
  • Veteran asset growth per completed cycle
  • Sensitivity to cattle-price scenarios

Land, feed & infrastructure

  • Near term — existing feedlot capacity and corn/ethanol feed products
  • Medium term — regional pasture capacity, irrigation-pivot grazing, silage, seasonal movement
  • Long term — intensive rotational grazing within corn/soybean regions, quantifying soil, yield, feed-cost and environmental effects
  • Any claimed soil-health or environmental benefit enters external materials only with agronomic evidence behind it
